## How It Works: The Two-Step Flow

Publishing a blog post through your website's AI connection is simpler than you might think. You:

1. **Tell your AI what you want the post to say.** Use your voice, a quick text, bullet points, a story — whatever's easiest.
2. **Review the draft, hit "approve," and it's live.**

That's it. No middle steps. No redirects. No "come back when you've written 1,200 words."

The magic is that your AI assistant now has real access to your website. Instead of Zapier telling you what it *can't* do, or a freelancer taking a week, your website just listens. And because every published post goes through your review queue first, you stay in control.

## Meet Sarah: A Real-World Scenario

Sarah is a Realtor in Denver who blogs about the local market every other week. She's not a writer — she's a salesperson who understands the Denver neighborhoods better than anyone in her office.

It's Tuesday at 2 p.m. A developer client calls her with a question about the South Platte office market. By the time she hangs up, she's thinking: "This is a blog post." She has fifteen minutes before her next showing.

Instead of opening a doc, Sarah does this:

**Sarah talks to her AI assistant:**

> "Hey, I want a blog post about the South Platte office market getting softer this quarter. The listing inventory is climbing for the first time in two years, which keeps sellers honest. But this isn't bad news — it just means less bidding war chaos. Write it for other agents who might not realize what this means for their clients. Make it friendly, not doomsday. And work in that rents are still climbing for tenants, so there's a split decision here."

The assistant writes. Two minutes, maybe three. Then:

**The assistant replies:**

> "Got it. I've written a post called 'South Platte Office Update: More Inventory, Fewer Surprises.' It explains the shift, why rents stayed firm, and what agents should tell clients who are nervous about timing. It's in your blog draft queue now, waiting for your review. Ready to see it?"

**Sarah clicks the review link**, reads for 30 seconds (it's good, maybe better than she'd have written it), and hits **Approve**. The post moves to draft-in-review. An approval email goes to her website. She says:

> "Publish it."

**Twelve hours later**, the post is live. Her Tuesday-afternoon thought is now a Tuesday-evening-published article on her website, indexed by Google, ranked by the time it went live. Wednesday morning, a phone call comes in from an agent who read it, asking about South Platte comps.

That's the flow. Let's walk through it step by step.

## Step 1: Have an Idea, Tell Your AI

You don't need a script. You don't need outline software. You just need to tell your AI what you're thinking.

**How to start:**

- **Talk it out loud.** Some agents send a voice memo to their assistant: "Real estate market is getting weird with interest rates, and I think agents in my sphere don't understand how that affects buyer power. I'd love a post about that."
- **Type a quick message.** "Write a post about why staging is worth the money even in a strong market. Use our listings as examples."
- **Share a thought, a stat, or a story.** "I just had three clients ask about recession—what does that even mean for real estate? Let's explain it."
- **Send a half-finished draft.** If you start writing and want help finishing, paste what you've got and say "Turn this into a blog post. Keep my tone."

Your AI assistant doesn't need perfect inputs. It needs your idea. And because your assistant now has real keys to your website (through the MCP server — think of it as your website's AI connection), it can turn ideas into real drafts, fast.

The assistant pulls information from your website if needed: past blog posts to match your voice, listing details if you want current examples, team information if you mention a team member. Then it writes.

**The output:** A draft post in your website's blog queue. It's *draft* — private, not indexed, not live. Only you can see the review link.

## Step 2: Review and Approve

Every post waits in your review queue before going live. This is the approval gate. It's where *you* stay in control.

**The process:**

1. Your assistant sends you a private review link. Nobody else can see the draft.
2. **You read it** (takes 2-5 minutes for a typical blog post).
3. **You approve or ask for changes.** 
   - Approve: "Looks good, publish it." The post goes live on your website.
   - Changes: "Make the tone less technical" or "Add something about first-time buyers." Your assistant rewrites, and you review again.
4. **Once it's live**, it's dated and indexed by Google. Your MCP server publishes it to your website with one command: "publish this."

The draft-first workflow means you can say "publish immediately" without fear. You've already read it. You've already approved it. It carries your voice and your accuracy.

## Why This Matters for Your Business

**Three reasons this workflow changes things:**

**1. Your blog actually gets updated.** The barrier to publishing—the time, the writing friction, the "I'll do it this weekend"—evaporates. You have an idea at 2 p.m., it's live by Thursday. No editor, no revision cycle. Because your AI assistant has real access to your website, turnaround is minutes, not weeks.

**2. You sound like you.** Your assistant learns your voice from your past posts (it reads them from your website). New posts follow your tone, your examples, your metaphors. Readers don't know an AI wrote it — they just think you're prolific.

**3. Your team can do this too.** If you have agents on your team, [getting your whole team connected](/team-rollout) means everyone can publish. Market updates from different neighborhoods, different agent perspectives, all one conversation. [Who controls what your website publishes](/auth-security) is up to you — you set the permissions.

**The business result:** You go from publishing six posts a year (because writing is hard) to six posts a month (because asking takes two minutes). Google loves fresh, regular content. Your SEO improves. Your website becomes a lead magnet instead of a placeholder.

## Getting Started

You need three things:

**1. An AI assistant that has access to your website.** [Connect Claude to your website](/connect-claude) in about ten minutes. If you use ChatGPT, [we cover that too](/connect-chatgpt-codex).

**2. An idea.** Could be a market observation, a warning, a breakdown of a local trend, a "frequently asked question I just answered three times." Anything.

**3. A moment to read the draft.** Takes five minutes. Then approve, and you're done.

The MCP server handles the plumbing. Your website listens. Your AI assistant publishes drafts. You approve once, and the post goes live. No Zapier workflows. No waiting. No friction.
